## Product — MVP scope

First version stays small. A feature set you can build in a week:

1. **Pairing + program.** You pick a "bro" (1:1, persistent). A shared weekly calendar — when to cowork, when to lift, when to meditate.
2. **Sync session.** Both of you start the timer at the same time. Cowork block (60-90 min) → lift block (45 min) → meditation/breath reset (5-10 min). Optional short start + end video check-in (Focusmate-style but brief).
3. **Body-aware blocks.** During cowork, the app surfaces your lift window. During lift, breath cues between sets. After meditation, a one-sentence log.
4. **Streak + accountability.** Auto weekly summary — who hit how many sessions, who ghosted. No romanticizing, just hard metrics.

**Anti-MVP (do NOT build):** social feed, public leaderboard, "discover bros" matching. The first version is a closed 1:1 product. Grow the tribe later.

## Risks — name them out loud

- **Long-distance cofounders die.** You wrote it yourself: "long distance doesnt work bc you should literally be side by side." Counter-argument: the product literally sells this exact pain. But operationally — 1 sync call/day, shared board, 1 deep session/week — will you actually run that with discipline?
- **Both of you are in solo-builder mode.** You: Unit + Blocker. Him: Eclipta. Cofounding either means putting your own product on hold or treating this as a **third** product. Which is it?
- **The "best friend → cofounder" trap.** Closeness ≠ operational fit. [[dostluk-yasatmak]] already solved how to keep this friendship alive — meaning this venture is **not the way to keep the friendship alive**. If you frame it that way, the product becomes an excuse. The product has to exist for its own sake.
- **Equity + time + location.** You're at Colonist (`work/colonist.md`); he's probably on his own thing. Where do the hours come from? How do you split equity? Don't bring this up on the **first** call — but don't dodge it either. Second call.

## First call — agenda

Don't pitch. Open with questions. If you try to solve your gap before seeing his, it stops being a partnership and becomes a monologue.

1. "What's been missing most in your life lately — a build partner, or something else?" (problem-fit)
2. "How's Eclipta going — solo, team, what isn't working?" (his capacity + his pain)
3. "If we built something together, what's the **own** problem you'd be solving for yourself?" (vision overlap)
4. **Then** lay out this wedge. Anchor it on the part of his answer that overlaps.
5. "Want to spend a week prototyping together — no commitment, just a test?" (low-stakes yes)

## Decision gate — 2 weeks out

The question to answer after two calls: **"Would I build this product alone if Bingen weren't in the picture?"**

- **Yes** → build it together anyway, but frame it as something that could survive if either of you walked.
- **No** → this isn't cofounding, it's wrapping the friendship in a project. Pull back — honor the friendship a different way, per [[dostluk-yasatmak]].
